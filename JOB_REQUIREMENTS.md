## Methodology

1. Sourced the canonical task domains for a Senior AI Governance / Risk Architect (level 50, AI Governance family) from the public references catalogued in `.aicg/job-requirements.json → authoritative_references`.
2. Mapped each task domain to (a) the role on our level ladder that should own it primarily and (b) the curriculum module that will cover it.
3. Applied the **ownership rule** — assign primary coverage to the lowest-level role that genuinely requires the skill; higher-level tracks link back rather than duplicate. In particular, deferred:
   - *Down* to [`ai-governance-analyst`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-governance-analyst-learning) (level 15) for operational analyst legwork (intake, inventory, framework-crosswalk drafting, first-draft impact assessments, model-card authoring at analyst-tier, control-tracking, regulatory tracking, reading eval / audit evidence). The architect *designs* the schemas and templates the analyst executes against.
   - *Down* to [`ai-risk-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (level 25) for the hands-on engineering craft of AI risk (harm-model authoring, red-team / adversarial-ML / fairness / privacy / guardrail engineering, quantification, MRM engineering, incident RCA). The architect *names* the controls; that role builds them.
   - *Down* to [`ml-engineer`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/ml-engineer-learning) (level 20) for classical ML fundamentals, assumed at the level required to reason about which ML system a control applies to; and to [`ai-infra-junior-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-junior-engineer-learning) (level 10) for engineering-craft prerequisites.
   - *Sideways-and-down* to [`ai-evaluation-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-evaluation-engineer-learning) (level 35, Governance family) for release-assurance / audit-trail / regulator-facing methodology depth. The architect *designs the assurance system*; that peer *executes inside it*.
   - *Sideways* to [`ai-eval-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-eval-engineer-learning) (peer, level 30, AI Engineering family) for application-layer LLM/agent evaluation-engineering depth (traces, judges, RAG eval, eval-gated CI/CD, online eval); and to [`model-evaluation-engineer`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/model-evaluation-engineer-learning) (peer, level 30, ML Engineering family) for benchmark / statistical / judge-vs-human calibration methodology depth.
   - *Sideways* to [`ai-infra-mlops`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-mlops-learning) (peer, level 25) for MLOps automation into which the governance-runtime slice plugs.
   - *Up-sideways* to [`security-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/security-learning) / [`ai-infra-security`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-security-learning) (level 35) for deep ML/AI security at platform scale — the architect integrates that peer's platform-scale defence into the enterprise control library.
   - *Up* to [`agentic-safety-engineer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (level 40) for frontier-agent red-team methodology and dangerous-capability evaluation. The architect *defines the tripwire / rollback contract*; that role *owns the technical methodology*.
   - *Up* to [`head-of-ai-governance`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (level 60) for program leadership, board-level reporting, and regulator engagement; and to [`chief-ai-officer`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) for executive scope and P&L alignment. The architect *hands the operating design*; those roles *lead it*.
   - *Out of scope* to legal counsel (this role does not deliver legal opinion — it packages regulatory obligations for counsel to interpret) and to security operations / DFIR (SOC-side incident handling; this role designs the interface, not the SOC runbook).
4. Flagged everything that has not yet been validated against in-window postings so the next cycle can demote any requirement whose evidence stays empty.

## Ownership map — quick reference for next cycle

When backfilling postings, use this ownership decision to keep the curriculum from drifting into peer territory:

- **AI Governance Analyst** (level 15, `ai-governance-analyst-learning`) — owns operational analyst work. **The architect designs the schemas + templates the analyst executes against.**
- **AI Risk Engineer** (level 25, `ai-risk-engineer-learning`) — owns the hands-on engineering craft of AI risk. **The architect names the controls; that role builds them.**
- **AI Eval Engineer** (peer, level 30, AI Engineering family) — owns application-layer LLM/agent evaluation-engineering depth.
- **Model Evaluation Engineer** (peer, level 30, ML Engineering family) — owns benchmark / statistical / calibration methodology depth.
- **AI Infra MLOps** (peer, level 25, Infra family) — owns MLOps automation into which the governance-runtime slice plugs.
- **AI Evaluation Engineer** (peer / lower specialist, level 35, `ai-evaluation-engineer-learning`) — owns release-assurance / audit-trail / regulator-facing methodology depth. **The architect designs the assurance system; that peer executes inside it.**
- **Security / AI Infra Security** (peer / next-up, level 35) — owns deep ML/AI security at platform scale. **The architect integrates that peer's platform-scale defence into the enterprise control library.**
- **Agentic Safety Engineer** (higher, level 40, `agentic-safety-engineer-learning`) — owns frontier-agent red-team methodology and dangerous-capability evaluation. **The architect defines the tripwire / rollback contract; that peer owns the technical methodology.**
- **Senior AI Governance / Risk Architect** (this track, level 50, `senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`) — owns the *architecture* of the enterprise AI governance and risk program: control library, policy taxonomy, cross-jurisdiction reconciliation, AIMS, risk taxonomy + appetite, assurance architecture, evidence architecture, third-party governance program, post-market surveillance architecture, GRC-for-AI reference architecture, operating model, sector blueprints.
- **Head of AI Governance** (level 60, `head-of-ai-governance-learning`) — owns program leadership, board-level reporting, regulator engagement. **The architect hands the operating design; that role leads the program.**
- **Chief AI Officer** (level 70, `chief-ai-officer-learning`) — owns AI strategy, P&L alignment, external positioning. **The architect hands the risk / governance foundation into strategy.**
