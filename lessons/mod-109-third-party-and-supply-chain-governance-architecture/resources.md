# Resources — mod-109 Third-Party and AI Supply-Chain Governance Program Architecture

Primary sources only. This module designs the enterprise third-party AI governance programme on top of several convergent bodies of primary literature: the Federal Reserve / OCC / FDIC 2023 Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships (SR 23-4 shape) as the process-shape reference the AI-specific programme extends; ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 8 and Annex A for the AIMS-level obligations on third-party-provided AI systems; the ISO/IEC 27036 supplier-relationships series and ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A supplier controls as the underlying information-security base; the NIST AI RMF (AI 100-1) MANAGE function and the NIST Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1) for the AI-specific risk categories; the OMB AI acquisition memoranda (M-24-18 and its M-25-22 successor) with the M-24-10 governance ancestor for the federal-facing acquisition shape; the shared vendor questionnaire tradition (Shared Assessments SIG, Cloud Security Alliance CAIQ) for the composition base; the software supply-chain evidence stack (CycloneDX with the ML-BOM profile, SPDX 3.0 with the AI profile, SLSA, Sigstore, in-toto, NIST SP 800-218 SSDF) for chapter 07's supply-chain evidence contract; the EU AI Act provider / deployer obligations that cascade through vendor management; and sector overlays where they attach (HIPAA business-associate discipline for PHI-flowing vendors, PCI DSS third-party discipline for cardholder-data-flowing vendors, FedRAMP / StateRAMP for federal-cloud engagements). Where a source is paywalled (ISO/IEC deliverables), the link resolves to the catalogue entry; the full normative text is behind ISO's licence and must be procured for authoritative use. Where a source is fast-moving (OMB memoranda under administration cycles, FAR AI-specific proceedings, SPDX 3.0 AI profile evolution, SLSA revisions, vendor market practice on AI-specific clauses), verify against the primary source at reading time and update the `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers in the chapter and exercise texts accordingly.

## Notes on citation currency

The OMB AI memoranda (M-24-10, M-24-18, M-25-22, and their successors) evolve on administration cycles and are the most volatile citations in this module — verify the currently-effective memoranda applicable to federal customers at reading time before treating the current mapping as authoritative. The FAR Council's AI-specific case openings, the SPDX 3.0 AI profile publication location, the SLSA specification revisions, the vendor market practice on AI-specific commitments (IP indemnity coverage, evaluation-access clauses, safety-evaluation disclosures), the ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A control text (which may be revised via amendment or an ISO/IEC 42002-family implementation guidance), the sector-regulator AI bulletins (NAIC adoption, Colorado SB 24-205 rulemaking, EU AI Act implementing acts, MAS FEAT successor guidance), and the foundation-model provider public materials are all revised on cadences shorter than this module's revision cadence. The chapters and exercises anchor to the editions cited above at authoring time (2026); readers reaching this module in a later year should confirm currency at reading time. The `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers in the chapter and exercise texts flag the specific spots where verification is most likely to be required. When a citation in a deliverable cannot be verified from primary sources at authoring time, follow the module's convention and mark it `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` rather than paraphrasing the source's content from memory.
