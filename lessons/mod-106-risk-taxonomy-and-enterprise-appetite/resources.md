# Resources — mod-106 Enterprise AI Risk Taxonomy and Appetite Architecture

Primary sources only. This module composes an enterprise-scope AI risk taxonomy and appetite architecture on top of several standing bodies of work: the NIST AI RMF family (as the US voluntary framework and its Generative AI Profile), the ISO 31000 / ISO/IEC 23894 risk-management family (as the parent risk-management framework and its AI-specific specialisation), ISO/IEC 27005 and COSO ERM (as sibling enterprise-risk-management frameworks the appetite architecture composes with), the AIRO / VAIR ontology work and the MIT AI Risk Repository (as reference corpora for candidate risk categories), the AI Incident Database and OECD.AI Incidents Monitor (as observation corpora for realised harms), and the publicly-documented frontier-lab tiered-risk frameworks (Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, Google DeepMind FSF, Frontier Model Forum publications) as shape templates the enterprise adapts. Where a source is paywalled (ISO and ISO/IEC deliverables), the link resolves to the catalogue entry with the title, publication year, and abstract; the full normative text is behind ISO's licence and must be procured for authoritative use. Where a source is fast-moving (frontier-lab framework revisions, MIT Repository updates, regulatory implementing acts), verify against the primary source at reading time and update the `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers in the chapter and exercise texts accordingly.

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework — the US voluntary framework (chapters 01, 02, 07; exercises 01, 05)

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1, January 2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) — the parent voluntary framework. Reads as a shape template rather than a prescriptive standard; the Govern / Map / Measure / Manage functions frame the enterprise architect's decomposition, and the Playbook (linked from the NIST AI RMF resource page) offers implementation suggestions per subcategory.
- [NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) — the GenAI-specific profile companion. Enumerates GenAI-specific risks — CBRN information; confabulation; dangerous, violent, or hateful content; data privacy; environmental impacts; harmful bias and homogenisation; human-AI configuration; information integrity; information security; intellectual property; obscene, degrading, and/or abusive content; value chain and component integration — and the corresponding actions across the four functions. Chapter 02's axis-1 (harm category) composition explicitly reads this list as candidate categories.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework — resource centre and Playbook](https://airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base/AI_RMF) — the current NIST-hosted portal for the AI RMF including the Playbook, use-case profiles, and adjacent documents. Track for new profile publications as NIST US AI Safety Institute activity expands.
- [NIST US AI Safety Institute publications](https://www.nist.gov/aisi) <!-- needs-research: verify the current NIST US AISI publication list at reading time; the AISI publishes evaluation methods and pre-deployment testing reports on foundation models on a rolling basis, and its outputs increasingly inform enterprise taxonomies. --> — the US federal centre for AI safety research and evaluation methodology. Enterprise architects reading tier-3+ capability evaluations should track AISI outputs as an evaluation-method input to the quantification contract.

## ISO 31000 and ISO/IEC 23894 — the risk-management family (chapters 01, 02, 03, 06; exercises 01, 02)

- [ISO 31000:2018 — Risk management — Guidelines (catalogue entry)](https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html) — the parent risk-management guidance the enterprise appetite architecture (chapter 03) is layered on. Not certifiable; guidance. Published February 2018. Paywalled.
- [ISO/IEC 31010:2019 — Risk management — Risk assessment techniques (catalogue entry)](https://www.iso.org/standard/72140.html) — the companion techniques catalogue. Useful for the calibration-workshop pattern chapter 06 sketches. Paywalled.
- [ISO Guide 73:2009 — Risk management — Vocabulary](https://www.iso.org/standard/44651.html) — the risk-management vocabulary the ISO 31000 family uses. Notably defines "risk appetite" and "risk tolerance" in the sense chapter 03 imports. Paywalled.
- [ISO/IEC 23894:2023 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Guidance on risk management (catalogue entry)](https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html) — the AI-specific risk-management guidance layered on top of ISO 31000. Published February 2023. Annex A enumerates AI-specific risk sources; Annex B enumerates AI-specific risk-management principles; Annex C touches AI risk assessment techniques. Chapter 02's axis-1 (harm category) composition reads Annex A as a candidate-category corpus alongside NIST AI 600-1. Paywalled.

## ISO/IEC 27005 — the sibling information-security-risk method (chapters 03, 06; exercise 02)

- [ISO/IEC 27005:2022 — Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Guidance on managing information security risks (catalogue entry)](https://www.iso.org/standard/80585.html) — the information-security-risk guidance the enterprise's ISMS runs against. Chapter 03 argues the appetite architecture must compose cleanly with the enterprise's existing 27005 practice for the categories that overlap (AI-adjacent information-security risks). Paywalled.
- [ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Information security management systems — Requirements (catalogue entry)](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) — the parent ISMS standard the 27005 method serves. Referenced by chapter 03 for the risk-acceptance-criteria shape the enterprise's ISMS carries and that the AI appetite architecture must be compatible with.

## AIRO / VAIR — the AI risk ontology (chapters 01, 02; exercise 01)

## MIT AI Risk Repository — the meta-taxonomy (chapters 01, 02, 07; exercises 01, 05)

- [MIT AI Risk Repository](https://airisk.mit.edu/) — a living meta-analysis of published AI risk taxonomies with an accompanying database of specific risks classified against a two-dimensional structure (causal taxonomy: entity / intent / timing × domain taxonomy: seven domains and dozens of subdomains). Chapter 01 names this as one of the reference corpora that must be composed from rather than adopted verbatim; chapter 02 walks the composition move. <!-- needs-research: verify current counts and version of the MIT AI Risk Repository at reading time; the repository publishes rolling updates and the specific taxonomy counts / risk counts cited in the chapters (43 taxonomies / 777 risks at v1) should be updated to current. -->
- [Slattery et al., "The AI Risk Repository: A Comprehensive Meta-Review, Database, and Taxonomy of Risks From Artificial Intelligence" (arXiv, 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12622) — the paper accompanying the MIT AI Risk Repository, describing the meta-review method and the two-dimensional taxonomy structure. Read alongside the repository itself.

## How to use this list

- **When authoring the taxonomy shape proposal** (exercise 01): the composition move reads NIST AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 23894 Annex A, AIRO / VAIR, MIT Repository, and AIID / OECD.AI as candidate-category corpora. Chapter 02 walks the composition; the URLs above resolve to the current publications each corpus maintains.
- **When translating the board appetite** (exercise 02): ISO 31000 provides the appetite / tolerance vocabulary; COSO ERM provides the appetite-through-strategy linkage; ISO/IEC 27005 provides the sibling-ISMS risk-acceptance shape the AI appetite must compose with.
- **When designing the aggregation model** (exercise 03): AIID / OECD.AI provide the historical-incident lineage for tail-view categories; FAIR provides the monetisation shape for the stretch view; the ISO 31010 techniques catalogue provides the alternate aggregation techniques the enterprise may draw from.
- **When adapting a frontier-lab framework** (exercise 04): read the specific published version of the framework you are adapting; the URLs above resolve to the current landing page for each framework, but the specific version cited must be pinned in your deliverable because frameworks revise on a cadence measured in months.
- **When authoring the versioning + migration plan** (exercise 05): the MIT Repository's rolling updates, the NIST AI 600-1 profile publications, the EU AI Act implementing acts, and the frontier-lab framework revisions are the principal sources of amendment-worthy change. Chapter 07's amendment workflow reads all four as legitimate environment-change sources.

## Notes on citation currency

The frontier-lab frameworks, the MIT AI Risk Repository, the OECD.AI publications, and the US federal AI governance memoranda are all revised on cadences shorter than a typical enterprise's taxonomy release cadence. The chapters and exercises of mod-106 anchor to the editions cited above at authoring time (2026); readers reaching this module in a later year should confirm currency at reading time before treating any specific version citation as authoritative.
