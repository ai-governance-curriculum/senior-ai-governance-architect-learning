# Senior AI Governance / Risk Architect Curriculum

**Role level:** 50 (architectural design authority — AI Governance family)
**Family:** AI Governance
**Status:** planned — modules and projects below are the planned scope authored from [`.aicg/curriculum-plan.json`](.aicg/curriculum-plan.json). Lessons and projects will be drafted by subsequent autonomous content cycles.

## Overview

This track teaches the *architecture* of an enterprise AI governance and risk program at level 50: reading NIST AI RMF (with Playbook + GenAI Profile), ISO/IEC 42001 / 42005 / 42006 / 23894 / 38507 / 22989 / 23053 / 24028 / 25059 / 27001 / 27005, ISO 31000, NIST SP 800-53 / 800-37 / CSF 2.0 / OSCAL, and the IEEE 7000-series as *architectural inputs* → designing an enterprise AI control library (control statements, applicability filters, implementation guidance, testing procedures, evidence contracts, machine-readable representation) that composes NIST AI RMF + ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A + EU AI Act + OWASP LLM Top 10 + MITRE ATLAS + Google SAIF + CISA/NCSC Secure AI into a single catalog → designing the enterprise AI policy taxonomy (Responsible AI principles → policy → standard → procedure → work instruction) with policy-as-code implementation (OPA/Rego, Cedar) → reconciling cross-jurisdiction requirements (EU AI Act + GDPR + CoE + OECD + UNESCO + G7 Hiroshima + US federal + US state + UK + Canada + China + Singapore + Australia + India + Korea + Brazil) into a single implementable target-state architecture → designing a certifiable ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS (scope, statement of applicability, risk-treatment plan, internal audit programme, management review) that a third-party certification body can audit per ISO/IEC 42006 → designing the enterprise AI risk taxonomy + appetite calibration + aggregation (ISO 31000 / ISO/IEC 27005 / COSO ERM as parent; AIID / OECD.AI / MIT AI Risk Repository / AIRO as reference corpora; Anthropic RSP / OpenAI Preparedness / Google DeepMind FSF / Frontier Model Forum as shape templates) → designing the enterprise assurance architecture (three lines of defense for AI, pre-deployment, ongoing, third-line audit) aligned to IIA Three Lines + SR 11-7 validation-vs-development independence + ForHumanity IAAIS + BABL AI shapes → designing the enterprise AI evidence architecture (model / system / dataset card schemas, audit-log architecture, ML-BOM + SPDX AI + SLSA + Sigstore at enterprise scale, OSCAL representation, regulator-facing artifact templates) → designing the third-party + AI-supply-chain governance program (SR 23-4 for AI vendors + OMB M-24-18 / M-25-22 for federal-facing AI acquisition, tiering, contract templates, ongoing monitoring) → designing the enterprise post-market surveillance architecture (EU AI Act Article 72 + 73, monitoring→risk-register at enterprise scale, deviation-from-appetite alarms) → designing the GRC-for-AI reference architecture + build-vs-buy across Credo AI, Holistic AI, ModelOp, Monitaur, ServiceNow AI Control Tower, IBM watsonx.governance, Fairly, Enzai, Trustible (with AI-runtime security and observability adjacencies) → designing the AI governance operating model (council charter, RACI, three-lines-of-defense for AI, role descriptions linked to the correct owner packet, hiring plan, communications + change-management) → publishing sector + jurisdiction blueprints (banking, insurance, health, pharma, public sector, critical infrastructure).

It is an **architectural design-authority track at level 50**, positioned in the **AI Governance family**, between the AI Evaluation Engineer (level 35, release-assurance methodology) / Agentic Safety Engineer (level 40, frontier-agent methodology) below and the Head of AI Governance (level 60, program leadership) / Chief AI Officer (level 70, executive scope) above. The differentiator against the lower `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25 — hands-on engineering craft) is *architecture ownership*: this curriculum designs the control library, policy taxonomy, cross-jurisdiction crosswalk, AIMS, risk taxonomy, assurance architecture, evidence architecture, third-party programme, post-market surveillance architecture, GRC-for-AI reference architecture, operating model, and sector blueprints. The differentiator against the higher `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) is that this role hands the *operating design*, not the *leadership execution* — the architect does not own board reporting, regulator engagement, or budget.

Total planned commitment: **212 hours across 13 modules** + **150 hours across 3 projects** = **~362 hours**.

## Ownership rule

Following the project-wide ownership rule, this curriculum:

- **Owns** the *architecture* of an enterprise AI governance and risk program — enterprise reference architecture for AI governance, control-library architecture, policy taxonomy + policy-as-code, cross-jurisdiction reconciliation, ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS architecture, enterprise AI risk taxonomy + appetite calibration, assurance architecture, evidence architecture, third-party + AI-supply-chain governance programme design, post-market surveillance architecture, GRC-for-AI reference architecture, AI governance operating model, sector + jurisdiction blueprints.
- **Defers down** to [`ai-governance-analyst-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-governance-analyst-learning) (level 15) for operational analyst legwork the architect designs schemas + templates for; to [`ai-risk-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-risk-engineer-learning) (level 25) for the hands-on engineering craft of AI risk the architect names controls for; to [`ml-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/ml-engineer-learning) (level 20) for classical ML fundamentals; to [`ai-infra-junior-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-junior-engineer-learning) (level 10) for engineering-craft prerequisites.
- **Defers sideways-and-down** to [`ai-evaluation-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/ai-evaluation-engineer-learning) (peer / lower specialist, level 35, Governance family) for release-assurance / audit-trail / regulator-facing methodology depth — the architect designs the assurance system that peer executes inside.
- **Defers sideways** to [`ai-eval-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/ai-eval-engineer-learning) (peer, level 30, AI Engineering family) for application-layer LLM/agent evaluation-engineering depth; to [`model-evaluation-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/model-evaluation-engineer-learning) (peer, level 30, ML Engineering family) for benchmark / statistical / calibration methodology depth; to [`ai-infra-mlops-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-mlops-learning) (peer, level 25, Infra family) for MLOps automation into which the governance-runtime slice plugs; to peer AI Engineering tracks [`llm-application-developer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/llm-application-developer-learning), [`rag-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/rag-engineer-learning), [`applied-ai-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-engineering-curriculum/applied-ai-engineer-learning), [`fine-tuning-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/fine-tuning-engineer-learning), and [`training-pipeline-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ml-engineering-curriculum/training-pipeline-engineer-learning) as the systems being governed at architectural scope.
- **Defers up-sideways** to [`security-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/security-learning) and [`ai-infra-security-learning`](https://github.com/ai-infra-curriculum/ai-infra-security-learning) (level 35) for deep ML/AI security at platform scale — the architect integrates that peer's platform-scale defence into the enterprise control library.
- **Defers up** to [`agentic-safety-engineer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/agentic-safety-engineer-learning) (level 40) for frontier-agent red-team methodology and dangerous-capability evaluation depth — the architect defines the tripwire / rollback contract; that role owns the technical methodology.
- **Defers up** to [`head-of-ai-governance-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/head-of-ai-governance-learning) (level 60) for program leadership, board-level reporting, and regulator engagement; and to [`chief-ai-officer-learning`](https://github.com/ai-governance-curriculum/chief-ai-officer-learning) (level 70) for executive scope and P&L alignment.
- **Out of scope** to legal counsel (this role does not deliver legal opinion — it packages regulatory obligations for counsel to interpret) and to security operations / DFIR (SOC-side incident handling; this role designs the interface, not the SOC runbook).

See [`JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md`](JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md) for the requirements-to-coverage map and the cited public references the catalog is grounded in.

## Module summaries

### mod-101 — Senior AI Governance Architect Role Scope and the AI Standards Landscape
Diagram this role's exact scope on the level ladder — what `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15) and `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) already own, what `ai-evaluation-engineer` (level 35), `agentic-safety-engineer` (level 40), `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60), and `chief-ai-officer` (level 70) inherit or extend, and where the architectural design authority begins and ends. Read NIST AI RMF 1.0 (with Playbook + GenAI Profile) as an *architectural input*, not a compliance checklist. Read ISO/IEC 42001 / 42005 / 42006 / 23894 / 38507 / 22989 / 23053 / 24028 / 25059 as the standards the architect writes against. Position NIST SP 800-53 rev5 + SP 800-37 rev2 + CSF 2.0 + OSCAL as the enterprise control-catalog + RMF-process + machine-readable-representation the AI control library composes with, and ISO/IEC 27001 + ISO 31000 + ISO/IEC 27005 + COSO ERM + COBIT 2019 + IEEE 7000-series as sibling frameworks the AI architecture plugs into.

### mod-102 — Enterprise AI Control Library Architecture
Design an AI control library — control statements, applicability filters (system tier / use case / jurisdiction / risk-appetite band), implementation guidance, testing procedures, evidence contracts — composing NIST AI RMF sub-categories with ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A, EU AI Act Articles 9-15, OWASP LLM Top 10 + MITRE ATLAS + Google SAIF + CISA/NCSC Secure AI System Development + ENISA multilayer good-practice into a single catalog. Represent it in OSCAL. Design the control-inheritance model, the control-authoring lifecycle, and the evidence contract per control. Author the delegation contract to `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) for engineering-side controls.

### mod-103 — AI Policy Taxonomy and Policy-as-Code Architecture
Design the AI policy hierarchy — Responsible AI principles → binding policy → standards → procedures → work instructions. Map every principle to at least one binding policy, one standard, and one testable control. Design the policy-as-code slice with OPA/Rego and Cedar — which policies enforce at runtime, which at CI/CD gate time, which by attestation only. Design the exception / waiver workflow and the policy-change communications flow that routes to `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15) for control-tracking updates and to `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) for engineering updates. Position ISO/IEC 38507 + ISO/IEC 22989 + IEEE 7000-series as the reference frames.

### mod-104 — Cross-Jurisdiction Requirement Reconciliation Architecture
Read the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) as an architectural input — Articles 6-15, 16-29, 50, 51-56, 61, 72, 73 — and locate each obligation in the enterprise control library. Layer GDPR Article 22, the CoE AI Convention, OECD AI Principles, UNESCO Ethics of AI, and the G7 Hiroshima Code. Read the US federal frame (EO 14179 with EO 14110 historical context, OMB M-24-10 / M-24-18 / M-25-21 / M-25-22, US AISI), the US state + municipal patchwork (Colorado AI Act, NYC LL 144, EEOC, CPPA ADMT + California SB 942 + AB 2013, Utah SB 149, Texas TRAIGA, Illinois HB 3773), and the international patchwork (UK Pro-Innovation Approach + AISI + ATRS, Canada AIDA + TBS Directive, China Interim Measures + TC260, Singapore Model AI Governance Framework + AI Verify, Australia Voluntary AI Safety Standard, India DPDPA + NITI Aayog, Korea AI Basic Act, Brazil PL 2338/2023). Design the reconciliation architecture — for each obligation, name the parent control, applicability filter, evidence contract, and deprecation path. Position CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 as the future-state harmonised-standards target.

### mod-105 — ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS Architecture and Integration
Design a certifiable ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS — scope statement, statement of applicability, risk-treatment plan, internal audit programme, management-review cadence, competence + communications plan, non-conformity + corrective-action process. Integrate the AIMS with an existing ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS. Integrate ISO/IEC 42005 AI impact assessment into the AIMS. Read ISO/IEC 42006 (audit / certification body requirements) and design an AIMS that a third-party certification body can audit. Position ISO/IEC 23053 as the reference architecture the AIMS scope is written against; ISO 31000 as the parent risk-management framework. Design the AIMS operations calendar and locate the operations owner (typically `head-of-ai-governance` at level 60).

### mod-106 — Enterprise AI Risk Taxonomy and Appetite Architecture
Design the enterprise AI risk taxonomy — canonical harm categories, capability tiers, dependency map — reusing NIST GenAI Profile categories, ISO/IEC 23894 risk sources, AIRO ontology categories, MIT AI Risk Repository meta-taxonomy, and AIID / OECD.AI incident classes. Design the enterprise AI risk appetite — how the board's risk-appetite statement translates into per-tier tolerances, escalation triggers, stop-shipping thresholds — layering ISO 31000, ISO/IEC 27005, and COSO ERM as parent frameworks. Design the aggregation model. Draw shape lessons from Anthropic RSP, OpenAI Preparedness, Google DeepMind FSF, and Frontier Model Forum publications. Coordinate with `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25) on the quantification contract and with `model-evaluation-engineer` (peer, level 30) on statistical calibration methodology.

### mod-107 — AI Assurance Architecture and Audit Readiness
Design the enterprise AI assurance architecture — three lines of defense for AI, pre-deployment assurance, ongoing assurance, third-line audit — aligned to IIA Three Lines, SR 11-7 validation-vs-development independence, and ISO/IEC 42006 audit-body requirements. Design the pre-deployment assurance gate (coordinating with `ai-evaluation-engineer` at level 35 on the release-assurance methodology that runs inside it). Design the ongoing assurance programme and third-line independent audit programme, using ForHumanity IAAIS + BABL AI + ISO/IEC 42006 as shape references. Design the interface with external audit / certification bodies. Position NIST SP 800-37 RMF as the reference process shape.

### mod-108 — AI Evidence Architecture and Documentation Schemas
Design the enterprise AI evidence architecture — audit-log architecture, evidence retention + immutability, chain-of-custody, storage tiering — satisfying EU AI Act, SR 11-7, and ISO/IEC 42001 record obligations simultaneously. Author model / system / dataset / risk-card schemas grounded in Mitchell et al. Model Cards, Gebru et al. Datasheets, Hugging Face Model Cards guide, Partnership on AI ABOUT ML, and the UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard. Design the AI supply-chain evidence slice at enterprise scale — CycloneDX ML-BOM, SPDX 3.0 AI profile, SLSA, Sigstore. Design regulator-facing artifact templates (EU AI Act Article 11 / 72 / 73, SR 11-7 model documentation, FDA PCCP submission). Represent the evidence-schema catalog in OSCAL.

### mod-109 — Third-Party and AI Supply-Chain Governance Program Architecture
Design the third-party AI governance programme — SR 23-4 shape at architect scope applied to foundation-model providers, guardrail vendors, AI-observability vendors, GRC-for-AI platform vendors, dataset providers. Design tiering criteria + due-diligence questionnaires per tier. Design contract-template controls (data-use limits, evaluation-access rights, incident-notification, evidence-access, exit / portability). Design the ongoing vendor-monitoring schedule. Read OMB M-24-18 and M-25-22 as the federal-facing AI acquisition shape. Design the AI supply-chain assurance slice at architect scope (ML-BOM + SPDX AI + SLSA + Sigstore), coordinating with `ai-infra-security` (level 35) on runtime supply-chain / signing platform depth.

### mod-110 — Enterprise Post-Market Surveillance and Monitoring Architecture
Design the enterprise post-market surveillance architecture — EU AI Act Article 72 at enterprise scale, incident aggregation into a single source of truth, near-miss handling, regulatory-reporting cadence, deviation-from-appetite alarms. Design the monitoring-to-risk-register contract at enterprise scale (extending the team-scope contract `ai-risk-engineer` at level 25 authors). Design the wiring across Fiddler AI, Arthur AI, WhyLabs, and Evidently into the GRC-for-AI system of record. Design the EU AI Act Article 73 serious-incident reporting workflow (coordinating with `head-of-ai-governance` at level 60 for regulator-facing filing). Design the SOC interface (coordinating with `security-learning` / `ai-infra-security` at level 35 on SOC-side depth). Layer AIID + OECD.AI + MIT AI Risk Repository as external calibration references.

### mod-111 — GRC-for-AI Platform and Toolchain Reference Architecture
Author the GRC-for-AI reference architecture — the AI governance system of record inside the enterprise stack, integrated with ML platforms, security tooling, enterprise GRC (Archer, ServiceNow GRC, MetricStream), identity, comms, ticketing. Evaluate the vendor landscape (Credo AI, Holistic AI, ModelOp, Monitaur, ServiceNow AI Control Tower, IBM watsonx.governance, Fairly AI, Enzai, Trustible, Verify AI, Cranium, HydroX AI) against the reference architecture. Position AI-runtime security adjacencies (Robust Intelligence, Lakera Guard, Calypso AI, HiddenLayer, Protect AI) and observability adjacencies (Fiddler, Arthur, WhyLabs, Evidently). Design the workflow layer (intake, impact-assessment, control-testing, evidence-collection, exception-handling, incident-routing, audit-facing packaging). Design the RBAC + SoD model satisfying SR 11-7 validation-vs-development independence and ISO/IEC 42001 competence requirements. Position Singapore AI Verify as the reference open-source assurance testing framework.

### mod-112 — AI Governance Operating Model and Program Design
Design the AI governance council — charter, membership, decision authority, escalation ladder, cadence, quorum. Design the three-lines-of-defense for AI operating model with clear RACI. Author role descriptions that link out to `ai-governance-analyst` (level 15), `ai-risk-engineer` (level 25), `ai-evaluation-engineer` (level 35), `ai-eval-engineer` (level 30), `model-evaluation-engineer` (level 30), `agentic-safety-engineer` (level 40), `security-learning` / `ai-infra-security` (level 35), `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60), `chief-ai-officer` (level 70). Design the cross-audience communications architecture (CISO / GC / CIO / CFO / CEO / audit committee / regulator / employee / customer). Design the change-management plan. Position the certifications portfolio (IAPP AIGP, ISO/IEC 42001 LI/LA, ForHumanity Independent AI Auditor, BABL AI, IAPP CIPP, ISACA CISA/CISM/CRISC). Design the standards-community contribution posture (ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42, CEN-CENELEC JTC 21, IEEE 7000-series, NIST public workshops, Frontier Model Forum, PAI, OECD.AI). Cite the boundary to `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) and `chief-ai-officer` (level 70).

### mod-113 — Sector and Jurisdiction Reference Blueprints
Author reference AIMS + control-library instantiations for six sectors — US G-SIB bank (SR 11-7 + OCC 2011-12 + SR 23-4 + EU AI Act + Colorado AI Act + OSFI E-23), US health system (FDA GMLP + FDA PCCP + HIPAA + EU AI Act Annex III + state medical-AI regulation), insurance carrier (NAIC AI Model Bulletin + Colorado + EU AI Act + state DOI), pharmaceutical company (GxP + EU AI Act + FDA + EMA), US federal-agency contractor (OMB M-25-21 + M-25-22 + FedRAMP + NIST SP 800-53 + NIST AI RMF + US AISI), critical-infrastructure operator (CISA/NCSC Secure AI System Development + ENISA + NIST CSF 2.0 + sector regulator). Design the sector-adaptation methodology (how to instantiate without forking the control library). Position Canada TBS + UK ATRS + Singapore AI Verify as adjacencies.
